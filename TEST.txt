CS121 Assignment 3 - Milestone 3 Test Queries
==============================================

20 queries used to evaluate ranking quality (effectiveness) and response
time (efficiency). Queries 1-10 performed well; queries 11-20 performed
poorly on one or both criteria. For each poor query we note what went wrong.

Setup: index built from DEV (55,393 documents, 1,066,436 unique terms).
Each query was run 3 times; the time shown is the median, measured
inside SearchEngine.search() (index already loaded). Top 10 results
were inspected for each query.

Every query finished well under 300 ms (slowest: 74 ms), so "poor
efficiency" below means slow relative to the other queries.


---------------------------------------------------------------
PART A - QUERIES THAT PERFORMED WELL (10)
---------------------------------------------------------------

1. cristina lopes                    84 matches, 0.3 ms
   GOOD. Her faculty profile is #1 and the rest of the top 10 are news
   items about her. Very fast because both terms are rare.
   Small issue: the profile and two news pages each appear twice
   (duplicate documents, see "Root causes").

2. ACM                               3,646 matches, 2.7 ms
   GOOD. Returns faculty publication lists (~eppstein, ~gmark, ~amirr1,
   ~majumder) that are full of ACM venues. Reasonable for a 1-word query.

3. donald bren school                3,046 matches, 9.8 ms
   MOSTLY GOOD. "About the Bren School" (#3) and Bren Hall pages (#4-5)
   are in the top 5. A 2007 news-notes page is #1 because it repeats
   the name many times.

4. informatics                       4,598 matches, 3.8 ms
   GOOD. Informatics people page, ICS faculty pages filtered by the
   Informatics department, and the Informatics degree page.

5. eppstein                          1,333 matches, 0.9 ms
   GOOD. All top 10 are David Eppstein's home and publication pages.

6. ics 31                            2,593 matches, 14.8 ms
   GOOD. ICS 31 lecture/course pages (Pattis, Kay) and the "ICS 31
   Credit by Exam" page. One bad hit at #1: a raw dataset text file
   (mondego maven-contents.txt) that happens to contain both tokens.

7. richard pattis                    111 matches, 0.5 ms
   GOOD. His home page is #1, his faculty profile is in the top 5.

8. hackathon                         238 matches, 0.2 ms
   GOOD. Hackathon event category page, Women's Hackathon, IoT
   hackathon news. Duplicates at #1-2 and news pages reachable by two
   URLs (view_news vs view_news.php).

9. master of software engineering    1,749 matches, 26.4 ms
   GOOD ranking: MSWE program page, curriculum, home, admissions, FAQ.
   Slower than other 2-term-ish queries: loading the postings for "of"
   (df 37,569, the largest list in the index) costs ~13 ms by itself.
   Duplicate of /grad/mswe/ at #1-2.

10. wifi                             74 matches, 0.1 ms
   GOOD. "WiFi at Mumbai airport", WiFi positioning dataset, "camera
   with wifi". Recall gap: 39 pages write "Wi-Fi", which the tokenizer
   splits into "wi" + "fi", so they never match "wifi".


---------------------------------------------------------------
PART B - QUERIES THAT PERFORMED POORLY (10)
---------------------------------------------------------------

11. machine learning                 4,419 matches, 9.2 ms
   POOR (ranking).
   - #1-4 and #8-9 are CML blog category listing pages
     (cml.ics.uci.edu/category/aiml/...), each appearing twice.
   - #5-6 are Pazzani's long publication lists.
   - No page that is actually about ML (e.g. the ML repository home or
     a course page) is in the top 5.
   Cause: duplicates take 2 slots each, and long listing pages with many
   occurrences win because scores are not normalized by document length.

12. data science                     5,045 matches, 14.9 ms
   POOR (ranking). Same CML category listing pages at #1-4 (duplicated),
   then news notes and Eppstein publication lists. No Data Science
   program/degree page in the top 10.
   Cause: same as 11 (duplicates, no length normalization, no
   phrase/proximity bonus for "data science" appearing together).

13. graduate admissions              2,073 matches, 4.4 ms
   POOR (ranking). #1-2 are MCS "once admitted" pages, #3-4 are the
   Informatics site map (duplicated), #5 is an ICS 1C homework page.
   The real ICS graduate admissions page (/grad/admissions/) is absent
   from the top 10 even though it appears for query 9.
   Cause: site maps and navigation-heavy pages contain both words; the
   important-tag bonus is a flat +2 whether the word is in the <title>
   or just in a <b> tag, so the actual admissions page doesn't stand out.

14. how to apply to the university of california irvine
                                     1,940 matches, 74.2 ms (slowest)
   POOR (efficiency and ranking).
   - Slowest query in the set. Loading postings for "of" (13 ms),
     "the" (13 ms), "to" (11-13 ms) and "how" (7 ms) is about half
     the time, for words that add almost nothing to the score.
   - Results are the CML listing pages (duplicated), a faculty home
     page and the internship page. No admissions page.
   Cause: stop words have huge postings lists that are fully parsed into
   Python dicts; long pages win because every common word adds a little
   score and there is no length normalization.

15. the                              30,945 matches, 25.8 ms
   POOR (efficiency and ranking). Every match is scored and sorted to
   return 10 results. The top 10 are WebDAV protocol drafts (RFC text),
   essentially arbitrary.
   Cause: a stop word carries no information (idf about 0.25), and
   there is no early termination.

16. machine lerning   (typo)         1 match, 2.4 ms
   POOR (recall). The only result is ~kay/wordlist.txt, a word list that
   happens to contain "lerning". All 4,419 "machine learning" pages are
   lost.
   Cause: strict AND (every term must match) and no spelling correction
   or fallback when a term is very rare.

17. cristina lopes software engineering research projects ubiquitous computing
                                     21 matches, 28.7 ms
   POOR (ranking and recall). Only 21 pages contain all 8 terms. Her
   informatics.uci.edu profile (#1 for query 1) is missing entirely; the
   ICS faculty profile is only #3, behind two news-notes pages.
   Cause: strict AND drops relevant pages missing any single word; long
   multi-topic pages (news notes, faculty listings) contain all the words
   and win.

18. ics                              18,691 matches, 14.9 ms
   POOR (ranking). Results are arbitrary course pages (ICS 45C project
   guide, ICS H197 syllabus, course updates). The ICS home page is not
   in the top 10.
   Cause: "ics" (stemmed to "ic") appears in 1/3 of the corpus, so idf
   is low and all top scores are nearly tied (2.25-2.30). The term
   matches in titles everywhere, so the important-tag bonus doesn't help
   distinguish pages.

19. computer science                 12,941 matches, 21.5 ms
   POOR (ranking). #1 is Eppstein's bibliography, #2-3 the duplicated
   CML listing page. The CS department faculty pages show up at #5-10.
   Cause: both words appear in navigation menus on most pages; no
   phrase/proximity scoring and no length normalization.

20. c++ programming                  4,200 matches, 11.7 ms
   POOR (ranking). Top result is Eppstein's publication file (pubs.ff);
   the rest are course pages for several languages.
   Cause: the tokenizer keeps only [a-z0-9]+, so "c++" becomes "c". The
   query is really "c programming", and "c" matches 8,604 pages
   (initials, grades, Section C, etc.).


---------------------------------------------------------------
ROOT CAUSES FOUND
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Duplicate documents: 1,601 URLs are indexed twice, so the same page
  takes two result slots (queries 1, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 19).
- No document length normalization: long listing/bibliography pages
  win (11, 12, 14, 17, 19).
- Stop words / very common terms: huge postings lists are fully parsed
  even though they barely affect the score (9, 14, 15).
- Strict AND with no fallback for rare or missing terms (16, 17).
- Low-idf terms give no ranking signal (15, 18).
- Flat important-tag bonus doesn't separate <title> from <b> (13, 18).
- No phrase/proximity scoring (12, 19).
- Tokenizer drops symbols and splits hyphens (10, 20).
